# Settler GTM Language Guide

**Classification:** Internal - Strategic  
**Date:** January 2026  
**Purpose:** Define language to avoid vs language to lean into for truth-aligned narrative

---

## Language Philosophy

**Core principle:** Reconciliation is a system behavior, not a human task.

**Language should reflect:**
- System-level enforcement, not human promises
- Deterministic guarantees, not "works most of the time"
- Risk elimination, not time savings
- Compliance guarantees, not vague promises

---

## Language to Avoid

### ❌ Productivity Language

**Avoid:**
- "Saves time"
- "Easy to use"
- "Simple setup"
- "Quick integration"
- "User-friendly"

**Why avoid:**
- Attracts price-sensitive buyers who want convenience
- Focuses on productivity, not guarantees
- Positions Settler as productivity tool, not system of record

**What to say instead:**
- "Eliminates $106K-$724K+ in annual risk"
- "System-level enforcement, not human promises"
- "5-minute integration, compliance-ready from day one"
- "Deterministic guarantees, not convenience"

---

### ❌ Feature Language

**Avoid:**
- "Look at all these features!"
- "You can do X, Y, Z!"
- "Feature-rich platform"
- "Comprehensive feature set"

**Why avoid:**
- Focuses on features, not value
- Attracts feature shoppers, not value buyers
- Doesn't explain why features matter

**What to say instead:**
- "Deterministic reconciliation: same inputs produce same outputs, always"
- "Complete audit trail: every decision recorded, reconstructible"
- "System-level enforcement: rules enforced, not suggested"
- "Compliance-ready infrastructure: SOC 2, GDPR, audit trails"

---

### ❌ Accuracy Claims

**Avoid:**
- "100% accurate"
- "Perfect matching"
- "Zero errors"
- "Flawless reconciliation"

**Why avoid:**
- No system is 100% accurate
- Edge cases exist
- Unmatched transactions require human review
- Collapses under scrutiny

**What to say instead:**
- "Deterministic reconciliation: same inputs produce same outputs, always"
- "Unmatched transactions surface automatically for review"
- "Exception supervision replaces manual matching"
- "95%+ of manual reconciliation work eliminated"

---

### ❌ Real-Time Claims

**Avoid:**
- "Real-time reconciliation"
- "Instant matching"
- "Immediate results"
- "Live reconciliation"

**Why avoid:**
- Webhook delays exist
- API rate limits exist
- Batch processing exists
- Not technically accurate

**What to say instead:**
- "Webhook-based reconciliation with near-real-time results"
- "Scheduled jobs available for batch processing"
- "Exception handling with automatic notifications"
- "Real-time visibility into reconciliation status"

---

### ❌ Compliance Claims

**Avoid:**
- "SOC 2 certified" (not yet certified)
- "100% compliant"
- "Fully compliant"
- "Compliance guaranteed"

**Why avoid:**
- SOC 2 Type II certification planned Q3 2026, not yet certified
- Compliance varies by industry and region
- Vague claims collapse under scrutiny

**What to say instead:**
- "SOC 2 Type II infrastructure ready. Certification planned Q3 2026"
- "GDPR/CCPA compliant exports"
- "Compliance-ready infrastructure: SOC 2, GDPR, PCI-DSS ready"
- "Security questionnaire available for enterprise customers"

---

### ❌ Integration Claims

**Avoid:**
- "Works with everything"
- "Any platform"
- "Universal integration"
- "All platforms supported"

**Why avoid:**
- We support 50+ platforms, not all platforms
- Custom adapters required for some platforms
- Collapses under scrutiny

**What to say instead:**
- "50+ platform adapters available"
- "Custom adapters available for enterprise customers"
- "Stripe, Shopify, QuickBooks, and 50+ platforms"
- "We maintain adapters, you don't"

---

### ❌ Manual Work Language

**Avoid:**
- "Manual reconciliation"
- "You need to configure"
- "Set up reconciliation"
- "Tune reconciliation"
- "User-managed logic"

**Why avoid:**
- Implies configuration burden
- Implies constant tuning
- Implies user-managed logic
- Contradicts core principle: "Reconciliation is a system behavior, not a human task"

**What to say instead:**
- "Reconciliation happens automatically"
- "Continuous reconciliation"
- "System-native behavior"
- "Exception supervision" (not "manual review")
- "The old way is structurally broken" (not "inefficient")

---

### ❌ Price Language

**Avoid:**
- "Affordable"
- "Cheap"
- "Low cost"
- "Budget-friendly"

**Why avoid:**
- Attracts price-only buyers who will churn
- Doesn't communicate value
- Positions Settler as commodity, not value

**What to say instead:**
- "7-42x ROI compared to building in-house"
- "Eliminates $106K-$724K+ in annual risk"
- "10-100x cheaper than enterprise solutions"
- "Value-based pricing: risk elimination, not transaction volume"

---

### ❌ Testimonial Language

**Avoid:**
- "Customers love us"
- "Rave reviews"
- "5-star ratings"
- "Happy customers"

**Why avoid:**
- Not structural proof
- Doesn't explain why Settler is inevitable
- Focuses on sentiment, not value

**What to say instead:**
- "Deterministic reconciliation: same inputs produce same outputs, always"
- "Complete audit trail: every decision recorded, reconstructible"
- "System-level enforcement: rules enforced, not suggested"
- "Compliance-ready infrastructure: SOC 2, GDPR, audit trails"

**Use:**
- "Deterministic reconciliation: same inputs produce same outputs, always"
- "No randomness"
- "Deterministic guarantees"
- "Same inputs = same outputs"

**Why use:**
- Structural proof, not marketing claims
- Survives technical due diligence
- Positions Settler as "must never be wrong" system, not "works most of the time" tool

## Language Validation Checklist

**Before using any language, ask:**

- [ ] Does it reflect system behavior, not human tasks?
- [ ] Does it communicate deterministic guarantees, not "works most of the time"?
- [ ] Does it tie value to risk elimination, not time savings?
- [ ] Does it position Settler as system of record, not productivity tool?
- [ ] Does it survive CFO scrutiny?
- [ ] Does it survive technical due diligence?
- [ ] Does it survive enterprise procurement?
- [ ] Does it attract right buyers, not wrong buyers?

**If any answer is "no," revise the language.**
